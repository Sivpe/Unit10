# UNIT 10 — CHANGING FASHIONS (Book 2, pp. 104–105)
## Teacher's Master Lesson Plan — One Double Lesson (90 minutes)
**Level:** Secondary 8 (Grade 8) · **Theme:** In fashion / In the picture
**Main aim:** Students can talk and read about changing fashions, using vocabulary for clothes
(parts, materials, look & fit) and the verbs *fit / match / look good / try on / wear*.
**Secondary aims:** listening for detail about decades; pronunciation of /t/ vs /d/; giving opinions about clothes; speaking about 2010s & 2020s style.

> **Phones in the lesson:** students work on:
> 1. **`01_vocabulary_worksheet.html`** — interactive vocab quiz (opens in any phone browser).
> 2. **`02_reading_worksheet.html`** — interactive reading passage + comprehension (phone-friendly).
> Share the two HTML files to students (or host them / send via class group). They need NO internet — they are self-contained.
> Each is a whole exercise set with a **Check** button and a score, so students can self-pace and self-check.

## LESSON 1 — Fashion Words & the 1970s–2000s Report (pp. 104)
*(Target: prep-of-class vocabulary + listening/reading comprehension)*

### ⏱ 0–5 min · WARM-UP — "Guess the decade!" (slideshow)
- Flash 4 images (flashy 1970s, 80s punk, 90s grunge, 2000s casual) — or just describe them.
- Students shout the decade + one word to describe the clothes (*bright, baggy, tight, plain*).
- **Hook question:** *"What are you wearing RIGHT NOW? Compare with your parent's photos."*

### ⏱ 5–12 min · BRAIN RACE (Ex 1a)
- Split class in 2 teams. 3 minutes: write as many **clothes & footwear** and **accessories** as possible.
- Points for each correct word. Bonus point for any part/material word.
- ⭐ **Fun upgrade:** "word tennis" — the teacher says *pants*, student says *belt*, next says *socks*…

### ⏱ 12–22 min · VERBS IN USE (Ex 1b)
- Teach with **TPR + personal examples** (wear the actual classroom clothes):
  - *fit* (size), *match* (colours go together), *look good on* (suits a person),
    *try on* (test in a mirror), *wear* (have on now).
- Students complete Ex 1b in pairs → **then open `01_vocabulary_worksheet.html` Section 1** on phones and race to finish. Check whole-class, peer feedback.

### ⏱ 22–35 min · CATEGORIES + VOCAB QUIZ (Ex 2 + worksheet)
- Introduce the word bank: *collar, cuff, sleeve, pocket, button, hem, belt, sole / silk, cotton, denim, leather, wool / patterned, plain, tight, baggy, loose, big, small.*
- **Word sort game** on the board: 3 columns — *Parts of clothes | Materials | Look & fit.*
- Students do the **same sort on their phones** (Section 2 of the vocab worksheet).
- ⭐ *Pronunciation note:* mini-drill of the target items.

### ⏱ 35–50 min · LISTENING / READING — "The Changing Fashions Report" (Ex 3 + 4)
- Play the audio (Ex 3) once **without** books: *"Which key word describes each decade?"*
- Second listen with **`02_reading_worksheet.html`** open: students read the full passage and
  fill the gap-fill (Ex 4) and True/False tasks on their phones.
- **Bank the key words: 70s = bright/flared, 80s = punk/shoulder pads, 90s = denim/grunge, 2000s = eco/eco-friendly & organic.**

## LESSON 2 — Pronunciation, Opinions & Speaking (pp. 105)

### ⏱ 50–58 min · PRONUNCIATION — /t/ vs /d/ (Ex 5)
- Model *tight/pocket/denim/patterned*. Contrast *tie vs die, coat vs code, feet vs feed, hat vs had, white vs wide.*
- ⭐ **Fun game:** teacher says a word; students raise hand for /t/ (make a "T"), both hands for /d/.
- Then the classic **"minimal-pair wipeout"** — call out *white/wide*, students point to the picture on phones, randomly alternate, drop out the wrong ones.

### ⏱ 58–70 min · OPINION PAIR-TALK (Ex 6 + 7)
- Model: *"I don't like the '70s shirt because the collar's too big and I don't like patterned clothes."*
- Students in pairs give opinions about the pictures — use *Because… / Actually, I like it. It's different.*
- ⭐ **"Fashion judge" mini-debate:** give one pair a "love it" card, one a "hate it" card; they argue over an outfit for 60 seconds.

### ⏱ 70–85 min · SPEAK/PRODUCE — "Futurist Fashion Show" (Ex 8)
- Brainstorm 2010s & 2020s fashion (yoga pants, athleisure, oversized hoodies, e-boy/e-girl, vintage/Y2K revival, sustainable fashion).
- **Group task:** design the "2030s look" — *name it, pick parts of clothes, materials, look & fit, accessories*, then present in 60 seconds.
- Vote on the best & most eco-friendly design.

### ⏱ 85–90 min · WRAP-UP — Exit ticket
- Each student writes ONE sentence answering: *"Which decade fits your style best, and why?"*
- **Bonus homework:** take a photo (or find one) of your parent's/grandparent's style; write 3 sentences about it, OR do **"Words & Beyond", p. 139** (Workbook p. 112).

## ✅ ANSWER KEY (for the teacher)

### Ex 1b (verbs)
1. I'm going to **try on** these jeans.  2. This T-shirt doesn't **match** this skirt.
3. These shorts **fit** really well.  4. I never **wear** sneakers.  5. These pants don't **look good** on me.

### Ex 2 (categorise)
| Parts of clothes | Materials | Look and fit |
|---|---|---|
| collar, cuff, apron, sleeve, pocket, button, hem, belt, sole, waist | silk, cotton, denim, leather, wool, organic cotton | patterned, plain, tight, baggy, loose, big, small, bright |

### Ex 4 (the 1970s–2000s report gap-fill)
1. Pants had **waists** high above the waist, were **tight** at the top but wide below the knees.
2. Shirts were worn with the collars **turned up**.
3. Pants were **worn** around the waist with huge side **pockets**.
4. Jeans, T-shirts with short and long **sleeves**, sneakers, and hoodies came on the scene.
5. Hair became smooth, and **dark** colours replaced bright **colourful** clothes.
6. Everybody from babies to grandparents wore **jeans**.
7. Elegant **patterned** shirts and soft **silk** scarves were combined with jeans and sneakers.
8. People became more eco-friendly and non-**leather** purses and shoes, and organic cotton started to appear.

### Ex 5 pronunciation (minimal pairs)
1. coat / code  2. feet / feed  3. hat / had  4. tight / tied  5. white / wide

## 🧩 DIFFERENTIATION
- **Support:** give weaker students the verb/word bank; let them open the worksheet first and re-try.
- **Challenge (strong students):** ask them to write *two* sentences per gap in Ex 4, or present the 2030 design in English only, no notes.
- **Fast finishers:** "Design a 2050 eco-outfit" or do the Book p. 139 Words & Beyond.
